## 1. Objective
An individual operates a productive asset and **compensates another individual for work** (end-to-end scenario 3): hiring by agreement, work at the employer's asset under a `Work` right, wages handed over physically, arrears when unpaid, walk-outs that suspend rather than end. Then (C3b) enterprises and cooperatives as organisations that hire.

## 2. User-facing outcome
The player sees a worker at someone else's bench, the output going into the owner's store, and the owner handing over goods at the end of a shift; an unpaid worker stops, gestures a demand, or leaves. The person card's Economy section shows the employment and wage history (data from `CivCard`, drawn by U5). Checkpoint C.

## 5. Allowed scope
**C3a (after C1):**
1. `EmploymentContract { int Id /*ContractId*/; Owner Employer; int Worker; int Asset; PayTerms Terms { ClaimUnit Pay; PayPer Per (Shift, Unit, Task); int ShiftTicks }; ContractStatus Status (Offered, Active, Suspended, Ended); long SinceTick; int ArrearsUnits; EndReason Reason }` in `CivState.C3`, ascending id; `ContractId` from `CivState.NextContractId`, only via `Employment.Hire`.
2. **Hiring by agreement**: the employer (an NPC with an asset it owns or controls) offers work by gesture (`Offer`, `TopicKind.Posting`, terms) to a perceived NPC; the worker decides through its own scores (pay valued by `Valuation`, minus effort, walking, risk and its own needs, plus learned bias); `Accept` → `Hire` (99) opens the contract and calls C1's `Assets.GrantUse(Work)`. Boards and postings (ECONOMY's `JobPosting`) are optional in C3a: face-to-face offers are enough for scenario 3; if postings are built, they are perceived only by reading a board or being told.
3. **`WorkFor` (100)**: the worker works the asset with C1's `Work` act during a shift; output is owned by the asset owner (C1 rule); job-day accounting (`JobDay` 253).
4. **`Pay` (101)**: the employer hands over the agreed pay within `Reach.HandOver` (basis `Wage`) at the end of each unit; **it may cheat** (choose not to pay); unpaid units accumulate `ArrearsUnits`; if C2 is merged, arrears open a `FormalObligation` with `Source = Wage` through C2's API (else a counter only, recorded as a default).
5. **Walk-outs (owner Q19)**: a worker who refuses to work without quitting suspends the contract (`Suspended`: no work, no pay due, slot kept, no abandonment timer); either side may resume, renegotiate (`Offer` on terms), resign or dismiss by its own scores. Nothing ends a suspension automatically.
6. **Ending**: resign, dismiss, asset destroyed, death (`CivHooks.OnDeath` C3 partial ends contracts with reason died; resurrection never restores them, D5.13), `Civ.C3.AbandonTicks` (default 300) without progress on an active contract.
7. Learning: outcome rewards through the normal channel (paid → the food or goods themselves; unpaid → no reward), `Feature` keys with the employer as `NpcRef`, episode kind 17.
8. `CivCard` rows: contracts held, terms as understood, arrears believed.
9. Scenario `DotNet/God.Runner/Scenarios/civ_employment.scenario.json` (scenario 3 fixture: an owner with a workshop and inputs, a hungry neighbour without an asset).

**C3b (after C6 merges):**
10. Enterprise and cooperative **org templates** on C6's framework: `OrgType.Enterprise` with `OwnershipModel.Private` (owner decides) or `Cooperative` (members are owner-workers, `MajorityPresent`), founded through C6's opportunity path; employer party `Org(id)`; pay by the office-holder with `Spend` power from the org store; output owned by `Org(id)`. Files: `Orgs/OrgState.C3.cs` and template rows added through C6's template table API.

## 22. Used later by industry (keep the interfaces general)
The industrial workstream will employ **operators of vehicles and harvesters, power-station and factory staff, miners, crews and engineers**, paid later in money (K2), by private firms, utilities, cooperatives, military organisations and governments (drafts: `docs/stage4/VEHICLES_AND_HARVESTING_DESIGN.md` §4 "Who operates" — a C3 job kind `Operate` and role `Operator` added by I2; `INDUSTRIAL_RESOURCES_AND_PRODUCTION_DESIGN.md` §4 workers; `MONEY_AND_ACCOUNTING_DESIGN.md` §12 K2-R6 — **C3's `Pay` verb 101 becomes the general payment act**). Therefore:
- `Employer` is any `Owner` party; `Asset` refers to C1's general asset id (vehicles included); one contract may cover a role on an asset with several staff slots (`Slot` index); the job kind is an append-only value set (so I2 can add `Operate`) and the role an append-only `Role`.
- **`Pay` (101) is written as a general hand-over act** with a purpose argument (`Wage` now), not wage-specific code paths, so K2 can route any payment (`PaymentPurpose`) through it; keep the act's start, reach check and completion in one function.
- `PayTerms.Pay` is a `ClaimUnit` (ware today; money later, see C2 §22), so a money wage adds a unit kind, not a new contract type; `Per` admits shifts and output units.
- Arrears go through C2's registry (`Source = Wage`), so K2 books unpaid wages as liabilities without a second system.
- Shift timing and abandonment live in tuning, not constants, so continuous plant operation can set its own shift pattern.
